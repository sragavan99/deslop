\begin{abstract}
We give a detailed exposition of OpenAI's construction for deterministic simulation of polynomial-time randomized logarithmic-space computation. The central result approximates arbitrary acceptance probabilities to any fixed inverse-polynomial accuracy in logarithmic space and polynomial time, yielding $\Lclass=\RL=\BPL$. The construction first transfers transition contributions into an accumulated reward while reducing the remaining transition mass. It then estimates the reward using sparse random tables described by logarithmically many bits. The main implementation issue is to evaluate the recursive estimator without retaining a full vertex address or numerical register at every level. We explain the conditional averaging, successive-accuracy estimates, row compression, and total memory accounting that address this issue. The quantitative accuracy result, accepting-computation construction, and effective compiler are included with their proofs.
\end{abstract}
